# Product

## Register

brand+product

## Users

Young urban adults (18–35) shopping for casual and streetwear clothing (sweaters, t-shirts, etc.) from their phones or laptops. They browse in their own time, often in the evening. They want a clean, confident experience — not overwhelmed by promotions or noise. Discovery matters: they want to feel like they landed on something with taste, not just another fast-fashion aggregator.

## Product Purpose

A direct-to-consumer fashion storefront. The homepage establishes brand identity and pulls visitors in before routing them to product listings. Success means a shopper lands, feels something, and adds to cart without friction.

## Brand Personality

Precise. Editorial. Effortless.

References: Zara (brand presence before the catalog), Arc'teryx (disciplined space, confidence in restraint), Ssense (cold-editorial, product as image).

## Anti-references

Shein — chaotic, promotion-overloaded, zero breathing room. Any site that leads with discount banners, neon sale badges, or packed grids with no whitespace. Also: generic SaaS-cream neutrals, stock photo hero sections, badge-heavy product cards.

## Design Principles

1. **Brand before catalog** — the homepage is a statement, not a grid. Give it room to breathe before the user reaches products.
2. **Restraint as confidence** — whitespace is not emptiness. Empty space signals quality.
3. **Product is the hero** — once in the catalog, the product imagery carries the weight. The UI steps back.
4. **Motion with purpose** — transitions and animations reinforce the editorial feel; they are never decorative or distracting.
5. **Consistent in both lights** — dark mode is not an afterthought. It should feel equally intentional and premium.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Standard reduced-motion support via `prefers-reduced-motion`. No specific user needs beyond that.
